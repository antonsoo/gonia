# Echea room: design and scope

The question is how a written musical prescription becomes an architectural
arrangement. The existing theatre room constructs the plan but makes no acoustic
claims. This room connects its geometry to V.5 without inventing a measured
ancient theatre or a recovered instrument.

The main object is a thirteen-place semicircle. Selecting one of seven named
pitches locates both matching vessels (one at the centre), links the Latin and
lets the reader hear the pitch. A flattened pitch trace exposes the reversal at
paramese / nete synhemmenon. An explicitly altered ascending arrangement provides
a comparison. A separate one-mode resonator demonstrates selectivity, using a
stated modern equation and freely chosen damping. It does not render theatre sound.

On phones, the plan is an overview; the seven full-width native buttons are the
controls. Labels in the trace become numbers mapped to the pitch list. No tiny
SVG click targets, hover-only facts or moving keyboard focus during playback.
Motion is confined to an audio playhead, disabled under reduced-motion settings.

Review: another generic architecture catalogue would not answer the question.
The distinctive element is the heard and drawn non-monotonic pitch row. There
are no statistics cards or stock photographs. The separate resonator panel must
say what its amplitude measures, and saved audio must preserve relative levels.
