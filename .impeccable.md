# Gonia design context

Audience supplied by the project owner: fluent English speakers curious about ancient architecture, especially Greek architecture, who want illustrations and ancient vocabulary alongside substantive learning. This is an open-source showcase, with an explicit preference for distinctive, important ideas over generic galleries.

The central task is to understand and manipulate one real architectural conflict: a Doric frieze turning a corner. No prior architectural vocabulary is assumed. A learner should see a change, understand its geometric cause, and read a relevant ancient passage.

Design direction chosen for that task: a precise architectural workshop. Warm plaster ground, deep green ink, vermilion for the discrepancy, generous measured drawings, spare technical annotations and serif source text. No simulated parchment, decorative temple photos, generic dashboard cards or marketing claims. Mobile keeps the corner readable through a deliberate detail view. All controls work by keyboard, terminology has English first, and uncertainty belongs beside the relevant claim.

The viewpoint room compares a source-prescribed 1:12 inclination with chosen observer positions. Keep the full-scale side view and enlarged angular detail distinct; native controls, visible rays, contextual Latin and a clearly labelled pinhole comparison form the workshop. The museum photograph identifies an actual capital, not an inferred inclination.
