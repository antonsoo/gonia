# Reading a measured column edge

The third workshop starts with Penrose's five-row comparison on printed p. 41
of the 1888 edition. These are corrected offsets from an axis-parallel datum,
not the original wire readings. The page also prints predictions for a
hyperbola and, in footnote 1, a circle through the end and middle stations.
All five stations remain visible. Lines between them are explicitly guides;
the application does not manufacture a continuously measured profile.

## Drawing and interaction

Keep Gonia's paper, green ink and burnt-orange dimension colour, Plex controls
and Noto Serif readings. Lead with the drawing, without a decorative temple.
An actual-proportion view shows one edge only: a complete column width is not
available in this table. A second view subtracts the endpoint chord and
enlarges the horizontal departures by a stated factor. Both views use the same
vertical scale. A movable station connects the dot, arithmetic and table row.


At 375 px the two diagrams stack at equal width, preserving their scale
relationship. Controls and numerical rows remain keyboard accessible.
The table scrolls inside its own labelled region. Shared URLs retain the
station, horizontal enlargement, candidate prediction, units and comparison.

## Evidence boundaries

- The maximum among these five rows is not the maximum of a continuous curve.
  Penrose separately reports 0.057 feet at 13.8 feet on p. 40.
- His p. 41 prose says differences do not exceed 0.001 feet; the printed middle
  row differs by 0.0011. Retain both numbers and describe the discrepancy.
- A better fit to selected points does not establish an ancient construction
  method, architectural intention, measurement uncertainty or an optical effect.
- Six-building comparisons use only the reported lengths and maxima on p. 40.
  Scaling maxima to the Parthenon's shaft length is a modern calculation from
  those rounded inputs. Preserve his separately rounded comparison column.
- The Roman text names entasis and promises a diagram. It supplies no
  Parthenon measurement or surviving numerical recipe in this passage.
- Use public-domain Heidelberg IIIF scans with their original attribution.
  Goriely's 2026 critique is a linked contrasting interpretation; no figures
  from that paper are copied or adapted.
